Privacy Policy

Effective October 23, 2026 · Updated October 23, 2026 (mailbox backups)

This policy explains how OpenMail (“OpenMail”, “the app”), a free and open-source desktop email application, handles your information. OpenMail is developed and maintained by the OpenMail project (GitHub: markdmac).

In short: OpenMail runs entirely on your own computer. Your email and account data go only between your computer and Google. The OpenMail project has no servers, does not collect any data, and cannot see your email.

1. Information OpenMail accesses

When you connect your Google account, OpenMail asks for the Gmail permission https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.modify. With your permission, it can access:

  • Your Gmail messages, including their content, attachments, headers (such as sender, recipients, subject and date) and labels;
  • Your Gmail labels and your Gmail address (to show which account is connected);
  • The ability to change labels on messages, move messages to Gmail Trash, and send email that you write.

OpenMail cannot permanently delete messages from your Gmail account. It does not request access to your contacts, calendar, Google Drive or any other Google service.

2. How OpenMail uses this information

OpenMail uses your Gmail data only to provide the features you see in the app:

FeatureWhat OpenMail does
Mailbox syncDownloads your messages and saves them into a mailbox file (PST) on your computer, organised into folders that match your Gmail labels.
Rules and clean-upApplies rules you create (for example “move messages from this sender to this folder”) and shows previews of which messages a rule would affect.
Moving mail to Gmail TrashOnly if you turn this on in Settings: after a message has been safely saved on your computer, moves it to Gmail Trash. Starred messages are kept in Gmail if you choose that option.
Sending mailSends messages you write, reply to or forward, through your Gmail account.

OpenMail does not use your data for advertising, marketing, profiling, analytics, or training artificial intelligence or machine-learning models.

3. Where your information is stored

Everything OpenMail stores is kept only on your own computer:

  • Your mailbox file (a PST file in the location you choose, by default Documents\OpenMail) containing the messages you have downloaded;
  • Optional backup copies of downloaded messages as .eml files (you can turn this off in Settings);
  • Optional mailbox backups: if you choose a backup folder and turn on “Back up on exit”, OpenMail copies your mailbox file into that folder when it closes (see section 4);
  • Settings and rules, and a list of Gmail message IDs OpenMail has already processed, so it does not process a message twice;
  • Your Google sign-in token, encrypted with your Windows account using Windows Data Protection (DPAPI). OpenMail never sees or stores your Google password.

4. Sharing and transfer of information

OpenMail does not sell, rent, share or transfer your information to the OpenMail project, to any third party, or to any server other than Google’s. No person at the OpenMail project can read your email, because the project never receives it.

Mailbox backups you set up. OpenMail’s backup feature only copies your mailbox file to a folder on your computer that you choose. If that folder is synced by a cloud storage service (for example Google Drive for desktop, OneDrive or Dropbox), that service, not OpenMail, uploads the copy to your cloud account, and its own privacy policy applies to the copy. The backup is off unless you turn it on, and it is off again every time OpenMail starts.

OpenMail communicates with Google over encrypted connections (HTTPS). The only other network connections happen when you choose them: opening a link in an email opens your web browser, and choosing “Show images” in a message loads that message’s pictures from the sender’s servers. OpenMail blocks remote images by default to protect your privacy.

5. Google API Services User Data Policy

OpenMail’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In particular, OpenMail uses Gmail data only to provide and improve user-facing features that are visible in the app; it does not transfer the data to others except as necessary to provide those features, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger or acquisition with your consent; it does not use the data for advertising; and it does not allow humans to read the data except with your explicit consent, for security purposes, to comply with law, or when the data has been aggregated and anonymised for internal operations.

6. Keeping, deleting and controlling your information

  • Your data stays on your computer until you delete it. You can delete your mailbox file, backup folder and settings at any time, or uninstall the app.
  • Choosing Sign out in OpenMail’s Settings revokes OpenMail’s access to your Google account and deletes the stored sign-in token.
  • You can also remove OpenMail’s access at any time from your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Messages that OpenMail moves to Gmail Trash stay there until Gmail deletes them (Gmail empties its Trash after 30 days). You can move them back to your inbox before then.

7. Security

OpenMail encrypts your sign-in token with Windows Data Protection, uses HTTPS for every connection to Google, and shows email content in a restricted viewer that blocks scripts. Because your mailbox file is stored on your computer, its security also depends on the security of your computer and Windows account. Because OpenMail is open source, anyone can review exactly how it handles data.

8. Children

OpenMail is not directed to children under 13, and the OpenMail project does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published on this page with a new effective date. Significant changes will also be noted in the project’s release notes on GitHub.

10. Contact

Questions about this policy or about OpenMail’s handling of data can be raised on the project’s GitHub issue tracker: github.com/markdmac/OpenMail/issues.
